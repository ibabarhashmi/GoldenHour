GoldenHour is a browser-based emergency co-pilot for the worst fifteen minutes of an Indian's financial life: the moment they realise they have been scammed. Today a panicking victim must find the 1930 helpline, which is often jammed, separately call their bank, and then fill a cold four-tab form on cybercrime.gov.in demanding a two-hundred-character description, transaction reference numbers and identity uploads, while their money moves further away. Police call the first hour the golden hour because that is when a transfer can still be frozen. Nothing tells a victim the order, the words, or even the clock. GoldenHour replaces panic with one calm screen. The instant you tap "I've been scammed," a live countdown starts and three ordered actions appear: call 1930 with a ready script, freeze and dispute with your bank using the exact words, and file on NCRP with the mandatory description written for you from three plain-language taps. It works in Hindi and English, one-handed, on a mid-range phone, and keeps working offline. Afterwards it tracks your acknowledgement number, computes every escalation deadline as a real date, and tells you honestly what happens next. We rebuilt the portal's real four-tab flow and its seven financial-fraud sub-categories faithfully, so the comparison is fair. Every identifier is synthetic and checksum-verified to be invalid. Unlike a prettier form, GoldenHour changes the order and the emotion of the first response, turning a frozen victim into someone taking the three actions that actually recover money, inside the window where recovery is still possible.
